# GridScalr 0.34.0

Three operation-card fixes for the I/O rail workflow, plus the scanned-drawing
and canvas improvements from the 0.33.7 development builds. No schema change;
the project-file schema stays 28.

## Operation cards

- **The straight-line guide survives an I/O rail row.** A pipe or control cable
  no longer jumps to the raw pointer angle when it approaches a row. The ortho
  guide now projects onto the nearest row and the vertex lands exactly on that
  row, while an explicit port or symbol snap can still end a segment at any
  angle and `Shift` still forces free drawing.
- **A real channel connection is one click.** Right-click a pipe or line near a
  rail row and choose **Connect to channel** (`Anslut till kanal`). GridScalr
  inserts the existing connection-dot symbol at the exact wire/row intersection
  on the wire's own drawing layer, as one undo step. Crossings you did not mark
  stay clean.
- **A wire can continue from either end.** Right-click within the hit tolerance
  of a polyline's first or last vertex and choose **Continue line here**
  (`Fortsätt linje härifrån`). The new segments are appended to the same item
  with a shared joint, so a run can end at one channel, continue to the next and
  mark both connections. `Esc` leaves the original item unchanged.

## Drawing canvas and scanned pages

- **The drawing no longer blanks and redraws while you pan or zoom.** The page
  raster keeps the last complete bitmap on screen underneath the new one, and a
  coarse whole-page underlay sits beneath both, so a zoom step or a pan never
  flashes the page white before it refills.
- **A held scroll keeps filling the drawing.** The raster schedule used to
  restart its 90 ms debounce on every wheel event, so a continuous scroll
  rendered nothing until it stopped and the exposed strip stayed empty for the
  whole gesture. It now renders at most every 140 ms during a stream, and each
  render only needs the newly exposed edge. Measured: the drawing stayed at
  100 % of its settled ink during a two-second held scroll (73.9 % before).
- **Tiling steps aside when a tile costs more than a whole crop.** PDF.js walks
  the page operator list for every tile, so on a dense drawing one 512 px tile
  measured 88 ms while a full crop measured 205 ms. After the first tile exceeds
  30 ms the tiled path is abandoned for that session and the crop is rendered
  once, which on the reported drawing cut the raster work by roughly four
  fifths. The raster cadence also adapts to the measured cost, so an expensive
  render is not repeated every 140 ms while the pointer is still moving.
- **A pan that stays inside the rendered crop no longer touches the PDF at
  all.** The canvas skips the render when the bitmap on screen already covers
  the whole visible page area at the same zoom and pixel density, and the crop
  margin grew from 70 to 300 screen px, clamped to the sheet so the fit view
  counts as covered. On a scanned page, ten short pans measured 0 raster
  renders instead of 9-10, and a five-second pan 4 instead of 62-70, with the
  total raster work down 85-91 %.
- **A heavy page fills during the gesture instead of waiting for the release.**
  The binary "the last raster cost more than 60 ms, so wait for pointerup" rule
  is gone. While a gesture runs, a raster starts at most every
  clamp(1.5 x the last measured cost, 220 ms, 600 ms), and the first idle
  schedule renders at once. On the 74 Mpix scan page every render in the
  measured window started while the pointer was still down, and the largest
  single long task stayed at 69 ms (70 ms before).
- **A scanned page keeps a whole-page bitmap per zoom level.** The canvas caches
  one page-sized image per page and zoom octave in memory (128 MiB, least
  recently used). At the level's own scale it is the exact image, so a pan past
  the rendered crop and a return to that zoom blit it with no render at all;
  between scales it fills the gesture and the sharp crop lands once when you
  release, so a pan no longer stalls while the pointer is moving. A level is only
  built when its whole page fits the level caps and can be as sharp as the view,
  so the settled image never softens. On the scanned test page a five-second pan
  at an octave scale went from 4 renders to 0, a zoom-back produced no render,
  the resting image was pixel-identical, and the cache held 89.5 MB of its
  128 MiB budget through twenty zoom steps.
- **Less allocation churn while panning.** The tile layer no longer creates a
  new composition canvas on every render, and the gesture layer reuses its
  capture buffer, so a held pan stops producing tens of megabytes of garbage per
  second. The tile budget is raised from 64 to 128 MiB so a DPR 2 viewport stops
  evicting bitmaps on every pan step.
- **A long drag keeps the fast blit instead of falling back to a full redraw
  per frame.** The refresh that keeps markups current used to arm the settle
  timer, so the gesture ended about 250 ms into a drag and every later frame
  redrew all items. It now keeps re-capturing and refresh is driven by both time
  (120 ms) and distance (30 % of the smaller canvas side). Measured at the same
  end position: a fast drag reaches 99.8 % of the markup pixels of a fully
  settled slow drag.
- **Markups fill in while you pan, not only when you release.** A gesture that
  keeps receiving input now redraws the markup layer for real at most every
  250 ms instead of blitting the captured raster until pointer release, so a
  wheel-pan stream no longer leaves newly exposed markups missing.
- **The interim image is the drawing at 45 %, not a 384 px page thumbnail.**
  When the previous raster does not cover the new view (a zoom out or a large
  jump) one coarse pass of the same crop is rendered and shown under the exact
  tiles. Measured on the demo project: the drawing is complete 102–116 ms after
  a zoom-out step, and the markup layer stayed at 74–100 % coverage during a
  two-second wheel-pan stream instead of dropping to zero.
- **Tiles are anchored to the page, not to the visible crop.** Every pan used to
  move the crop origin, which changed every tile key and forced a full
  re-render of the visible drawing. The grid now uses page-space columns and
  rows at the current render scale, so a pan keeps every tile that is still on
  screen and renders only the newly exposed edge. Markups were already reused
  during a gesture; the drawing behind them now behaves the same way.

## Unchanged

- The raster at rest is still rendered at the exact zoom and device scale, so a
  settled view is pixel-identical to the previous release.
- The 128 MiB tile budget, the full-page fallback when `createImageBitmap` or
  `OffscreenCanvas` is missing, and the read-only reference, comparison and
  split-view paths.
- Everything from 0.33.0: the grouped File menu, **Send by e-mail**, and the
  measured performance work (p95 edit, gesture redraw and raster time).

## Verification baseline

- Accessibility: 7 palettes × 19 surfaces plus 5 Swedish checks (138 checks),
  0 serious or critical violations and 0 lower-impact findings reported.
- Touch targets: 757 visible controls across 23 surfaces, all at least 24 × 24
  CSS px with a coarse pointer.
- UI scale: 100 %, 150 % and 200 %, no overflow; the smallest canvas width was
  338 px at 200 %.
- Performance reference profile: 150 pages, 15 000 items, 60 images, 6.9 MB
  serialized project. p95 edit 61.9 ms, project open 39.7 ms, autosave 998.4 ms,
  page switch 38.8 ms, UI freeze 197.8 ms.
- Visual regression: 4 screens, 6 differing pixels at the 300 px / 12 channel
  tolerance.

## Verify

On an operation card, draw a cable down to a rail row and confirm the guide stays
straight and the endpoint sits on the row. Right-click a wire near one row and
mark the connection, then continue the wire to the next row and mark that one
too; each dot and the continuation are one undo step. Pan across a drawing and
zoom in and out. The drawing should stay visible and
move continuously, sharpening as the new tiles arrive, with no white flash and
no full redraw on every pointer move. On a scanned drawing, a short pan should
not re-render the page at all, and a longer drag should fill the newly exposed
strip while you are still dragging instead of after you release.
