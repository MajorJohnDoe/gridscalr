GridScalr and Excel: when is a spreadsheet enough?

A spreadsheet is excellent at arithmetic and poor at remembering where a number came from. The question is which of those your job needs.

Short answer

A spreadsheet is enough for a one-off list whose quantities you already trust. GridScalr is for quantities that must stay traceable to a page, a measurement and a revision. Both can export CSV, and GridScalr can import edited values back by stable ID.

The question behind the comparison

“Excel or GridScalr” is really two questions: where do the quantities come from, and what happens when the drawing changes? A spreadsheet can hold any list and any formula. What it cannot do on its own is prove that a row still matches the drawing it was copied from.

That difference matters more on recurring work than on a single order. The comparison below looks at traceability, deductions, revision handling and the handover between the two, not at which product has more features.

Criteria in brief

Criterion GridScalr A spreadsheet
Primary task Measure and take off quantities in PDF and TIF drawings Calculate and organise values you already have
Traceability Each quantity derives from a measured item on a page Depends on how the sheet was filled in
Deductions Separate measurements, subtracted inside the category and room A formula or a manually adjusted cell
Revision change Measurements keep their page and scale; a new revision can be compared Cells must be updated by hand
Recalculation Order, table, CSV and PDF read the same items Every formula and copy must stay consistent
Handover CSV and Excel exports derived from the items Native format, familiar to most buyers

GridScalr does not import XLSX. The CSV round trip updates existing rows by stable ID; it does not create new measurements from spreadsheet rows.

When a spreadsheet is enough

A spreadsheet is the better tool when the quantities are already decided, the job happens once, and nobody will ask where a number came from six months later. A short material list for a single repair, a budget built from rates you maintain elsewhere, or a template the procurement team already owns are all reasonable spreadsheet jobs.

It is also the right place for prices, discounts and commercial terms. GridScalr records quantities and units; it is not an estimating or accounting system, and it should not pretend to be one.

When the drawing is the source

GridScalr earns its place when the quantity has to survive a question. The measurement is linked to a page, a category and a room; the deduction is its own object; the order dialog shows gross, deduction, net, waste and order quantity from the same items that feed the table and the report. When a drawing is reissued, the page keeps its identity and the change can be reviewed.

That is also why the app refuses to guess. A missing scale, a hidden layer or a room without an area is reported rather than silently turned into a plausible number.

Moving between them

The handover is deliberate in both directions. Order CSV, Order Excel and Order PDF are exports of the same item data; the Excel workbook adds summary, category, room, page and markup sheets. A takeoff table can also export CSV for editing and import it back: GridScalr shows a dry run, applies only existing rows by stable ID, and treats reference and derived columns as read-only.

So the practical split is: measure and take off in GridScalr, then let the buyer sort, filter and price in the spreadsheet. Keep the project as the source, and do not maintain a second quantity list that nobody can trace.

Common mistakes

  • Copying quantities into a spreadsheet and then editing only the spreadsheet, so the two drift apart.
  • Using a formula for deductions that nobody can trace back to a measured shaft.
  • Expecting an XLSX file to import back into GridScalr; only the takeoff-table CSV round trip is supported.
  • Treating GridScalr as an estimating tool for prices and commercial terms.

Questions

Can I export quantities to Excel?

Yes. Order Excel writes a workbook with summary, category, room, page and markup sheets, all derived from the measured items. Order CSV and Order PDF export the same data.

Can I edit in Excel and import the result?

Yes, through a takeoff table CSV. The dry run shows changed and rejected rows, and only existing measurements and spaces are updated by stable ID.

Does GridScalr replace a spreadsheet?

No. It replaces the part where quantities are read from a drawing. Prices, rates and commercial terms still belong in the spreadsheet or the procurement system.
