Gross area and net area: what is the difference?

The gross is what the drawing shows. The net is what you can order. The gap is made of deductions, and it has to be deliberate.

Short answer

Gross area is the full measured area of a space. Net area is the gross minus deductions such as shafts, openings and other areas that will not be ordered. GridScalr keeps both visible, subtracts the deduction inside the same category and room, and applies waste to the net.

The terms in plain language

In everyday Swedish use, BTA (bruttoarea) is the gross floor area inside the external walls, and BOA (boarea) is the living area within it. English drawings use gross and net in much the same way: one figure describes the whole enclosed space, the other describes what the space can be used or ordered for.

The statutory definitions have exceptions and belong to the Swedish area standard, not to this app. GridScalr does not calculate a BTA or BOA classification. It records your gross measurement, your deductions and the arithmetic between them, and leaves the classification to the person who owns it.

Why deductions exist

A deduction is an area you measured but do not want in the order. A lift shaft, a stair opening, a duct riser or a void above a suspended ceiling all take space without taking material. If they stay in the gross, the order is too large; if they are deleted, the drawing no longer shows what was measured.

GridScalr therefore keeps the deduction as its own measurement marked Deduct. It is subtracted inside the same category and room, so the gross stays readable and the net stays explainable. A deduction that exceeds its gross is clamped to zero and the row shows a warning rather than a negative quantity.

Record the gross and the deduction

Draw the full area first, then switch Area mode to Deduct in the measurement flyout and draw the shaft or opening. Use the same category and room as the gross measurement, because a deduction only offsets inside its own group. Give the deduction a label such as Shaft 01 so the next reader knows why it is there.

This is deliberately two measurements rather than one polygon with a hole. The gross stays visible in the quantity report, the deduction can be reviewed on its own, and if the shaft is moved or resized later, the deduction stays a separate row.

From net to order

The order dialog reads the items, not a separate spreadsheet. Each row shows gross, deduction, net, waste and order quantity for one category, room and measurement type. Waste is calculated on the net, never on the gross, because material is bought for the area that will actually be covered.

Hidden items and hidden layers are excluded from the calculation, while locked but visible items are included. That is the same rule the canvas, the Markups CSV and every export use, so a quantity cannot quietly disagree with the drawing.

Check before you export

Before the order leaves the app, check four things. Every gross area has the deductions that belong to it; each deduction sits in the same category and room; no row shows a clamped deduction warning; and waste and rounding are set on the material that needs them.

The takeoff table and the PDF report read the same rows as the order dialog. If the numbers look wrong in one place, they are wrong everywhere, which is the point: there is one source and no second copy to reconcile.

Common mistakes

  • Deducting in another room or category, where the subtraction has no effect.
  • Applying waste to the gross area instead of the net.
  • Deleting the shaft from the drawing instead of measuring it as a deduction, so the gross is no longer reviewable.
  • Presenting the net as a statutory BOA figure without applying the standard's own rules.

Questions

Is net area the same as BOA?

Not automatically. BOA is a statutory living-area term with its own rules. Net area here is your gross minus your deductions, and it is comparable to BOA only when the measurement and the deductions follow the same rules.

Where is the deduction subtracted?

Inside the same category and room as the gross measurement. A deduction in another group does not reduce the row you are looking at.

Does waste apply to the gross or the net?

The net. Waste and rounding are applied to the quantity that will actually be installed or covered, after deductions.
