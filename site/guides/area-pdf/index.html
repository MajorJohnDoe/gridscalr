How to measure area in a PDF drawing

From calibration to net area and quantity — the workflow that gives the same answer twice.

Short answer

Calibrate the page against a known dimension or the printed scale, draw the area as a rectangle or polygon, mark shafts and openings as deductions, and link the measurement to the room. The net area recalculates automatically and follows into the quantity.

Prepare the drawing

You need the drawing as a PDF or a scanned TIF, a known dimension, and a decision on which rooms to measure. A scan needs enough resolution to read the dimensions and wall lines. GridScalr imports a TIF as pages, so it does not have to be converted first.

Calibrate the page

Calibrate before you measure anything. The calibration is the only step that affects every measurement on the page.

  1. Measure a known dimension, such as the 10 m calibration line on the demo plan.
  2. Check the result against a second dimension.
  3. Lock the scale so it cannot change by accident.

Draw the area

Use a rectangle for square rooms and a polygon for L-shapes, angled walls and shafts. With snapping on, the corners land on the drawing's lines, so the same room measures the same on two attempts.

The label shows the area and unit. Drag it clear of nearby geometry, or lock it inside a small room so it stays readable.

Subtract and link to the room

Shafts, openings and other areas that should not be ordered are marked as deductions. A deduction is a separate measurement that is subtracted from the net, so the gross area stays visible.

Link the measurement to the room in the space register. The room then sums its linked measurements, and a changed measurement updates the room area.

Check the net and export

Before exporting, check that every room has an area, that deductions sit in the right room, and that waste and rounding apply to the right material. The order dialog shows gross, deduction, net, waste and order quantity per room. The same numbers appear in the schedule and the report.

Common mistakes

  • Calibrating one sheet and assuming the rest follow. The scale is per page.
  • Putting the deduction in another room, so the gross stays too high.
  • Measuring before a recalibration. Each measurement keeps the scale it was created with.
  • Leaving a room without an area. It is flagged instead of showing a partial sum.

Questions

Do I need to calibrate to measure area?

Yes. Use a known dimension or the printed scale. Counts do not need a scale, but areas do.

Can I measure a scanned drawing?

Yes. TIF scans import as pages and are calibrated the same way as a PDF.

Does the room area update automatically?

Yes. The room sums its linked measurements, and deductions are subtracted from the net.
