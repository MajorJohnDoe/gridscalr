What is quantity takeoff?

The step between a drawing and a purchase order, and why every quantity should point back to a page.

Short answer

Quantity takeoff is the step between a drawing and a purchase order: you measure lengths, areas and counted objects, subtract what should not be ordered, and add waste and rounding. The result is a traceable list of quantities that a buyer or contractor can act on.

What quantity takeoff means

Quantity takeoff, or mängdning in Swedish, is the work of reading a drawing and recording how much of each thing the job needs. A length of pipe, a floor area, a number of doors: each is measured once and given a unit, a category and a room. The takeoff is not the drawing and not the invoice. It is the reasoned bridge between them, and it should be possible to point at any row and see which measurement produced it.

Who does the takeoff

Operations technicians take off what they will install, maintain or inspect. Technical property managers take off what procurement must buy, often across several buildings and floors. Contractors and quantity surveyors do the same work with their own tools. The common requirement is traceability: a quantity that cannot be traced back to a page and a measurement is hard to defend when the order is questioned.

What gets measured

Most takeoffs combine four kinds of data. Length covers pipes, cable routes and skirting. Area covers floors, walls and ceilings, with shafts and openings recorded as deductions. Counts cover doors, windows, radiators and fixtures, optionally with pieces per object. Volume covers spaces that need a depth as well as a footprint. Waste and rounding are rules applied per object or category, not a correction added to the report at the end.

From measurement to order

In GridScalr the takeoff lives in the same project as the drawing. Measurements are grouped by category and room, deductions stay visible next to the gross, and the order dialog shows gross, deduction, net, waste and order quantity. The same numbers feed the schedule, the CSV, the Excel workbook and the PDF report, because all of them are derived from the measured items. Nothing is copied into a second spreadsheet that can drift.

What a takeoff is not

A takeoff is not a price. It records quantities and units; rates, discounts and commercial terms belong in the procurement system. Nor is it a replacement for the drawing's own dimension notes. The drawing is the source, the takeoff is a structured reading of it, and the project file keeps the reading reviewable.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the takeoff as a second drawing that can be edited independently.
  • Adding waste to the gross area instead of the net.
  • Leaving a category or room unassigned, so the quantity cannot be reviewed.
  • Copying numbers into a spreadsheet without keeping the link to the measurement.

Questions

Is quantity takeoff the same as estimating?

No. Takeoff records quantities and units. Estimating adds prices, labour rates and commercial terms on top of those quantities.

Who usually performs a takeoff?

Operations technicians, technical property managers, contractors and quantity surveyors. The role decides which quantities matter, not the method.

Can I do a takeoff without a calibrated drawing?

You can count objects, but lengths, areas and volumes need a known scale. Calibrate the page first or enter the printed scale.
