Calibrate a drawing: 1:50, 1:100 and 1:400

The scale decides every length, area and volume. Calibrate once, check twice and lock the sheet.

Short answer

Measure a known dimension on the drawing or enter the printed scale. Check the result against a second dimension, and lock the scale when it is right. The scale belongs to one sheet, and each measurement keeps the scale it was created with.

Pick the reference

A dimension line on the drawing is the safest reference, because it reflects how the sheet was produced. The printed scale is faster but depends on the PDF not having been resized. When both are available, use the dimension line and keep the scale as a cross-check.

Measure the known dimension

Zoom in and place the two points on the ends of the dimension line. Snapping helps, but a line that has been redrawn or scaled can be slightly off; compare with a wall or a door before you trust it.

Enter or derive the scale

Enter the known length and unit, and the app derives millimetres per point. You can also type the printed scale directly. A sheet with several scales needs one calibration per view, because the scale is stored per page and per measurement.

Check and lock

Measure a second known dimension. If the two disagree, the reference is wrong or the sheet is distorted. When they agree, lock the scale so a stray drag cannot change it.

When the scale does not fit

Three causes cover most cases: the PDF was printed with "fit to page", the scan was resized, or the dimension belongs to another view on the same sheet. Check the drawing's own note, recalibrate, and remember that existing measurements keep their original scale.

Common mistakes

  • Calibrating on a sheet that was printed with fit-to-page.
  • Trusting a dimension line that was redrawn at another scale.
  • Assuming one calibration covers every sheet in the set.
  • Recalibrating without deciding whether existing measurements should follow.

Questions

What does 1:100 mean?

One millimetre on the drawing equals 100 millimetres in the building. At 1:50 the same line covers half the real length.

Can different sheets have different scales?

Yes. The scale is stored per page, which is common in a set with details and site plans.

What happens to existing measurements after recalibration?

They keep the scale they were created with. Updating them is a separate, deliberate action.
