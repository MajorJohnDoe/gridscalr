Measure area on a drawing — with deductions and rooms

Calibrate once, draw the room and subtract what should not be ordered. The net area follows the room, so a changed measurement updates the takeoff.

How it works

Calibrate, draw, subtract

  1. 1

    Calibrate the page

    Measure a known dimension or enter the printed 1:N scale. Check against a second dimension and lock the scale to the sheet.

  2. 2

    Draw the area

    Use a rectangle for square rooms and a polygon for L-shapes and angled walls. Snapping keeps the corners on the drawing's lines.

  3. 3

    Subtract and link

    Mark shafts and openings as deductions, then link the measurement to the room so the room area recalculates.

What the measurement carries

  • Scale Known dimension or printed 1:N, lockable per page
  • Area Rectangle and polygon with snapping
  • Deduction A separate row subtracted from net
  • Room Area recalculates from linked measurements

Common mistakes

  • Calibrating one sheet and assuming the rest. The scale is per page.
  • Putting the deduction in another room, so the gross stays too high.
  • Measuring before a recalibration. Each measurement keeps the scale it was created with; update deliberately.
  • Leaving a room with a missing area. It is flagged instead of showing a partial sum.

Questions

Questions

Do I need to calibrate before measuring area?

Yes. Use a known dimension or the printed scale. Counts do not need a scale, but areas do.

Can I measure a scanned drawing?

Yes. TIF scans import as pages and are calibrated the same way as a PDF.

Does the room area update when I change a measurement?

Yes. The room sums its linked measurements, and deductions are subtracted from the net.
